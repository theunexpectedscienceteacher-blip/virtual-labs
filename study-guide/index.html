Speed & Velocity 7.7A, 7.7B

average speed = total distance ÷ total time  units: m/s, km/h

Example: 120 m in 20 s → 120 ÷ 20 = 6 m/s

Whole trip: add ALL distance, add ALL time, then divide. Don't average the speeds!

Speed vs. velocity

  • Velocity = speed + direction (12 m/s west)
  • Same speed, new direction (turning) → velocity changes
  • Distance = total path. Displacement = start to finish, with direction.
    30 m east, then 10 m west → distance 40 m, displacement 20 m east

Distance-Time Graphs 7.7C

  • Straight sloped line = constant speed
  • Speed in a section = distance change ÷ time change
    (rose 20 m in 4 s → 5 m/s)
  • Time goes on the x-axis, distance on the y-axis

Net Force & Newton's 1st Law 7.7D

  • Same direction → add: 10 N right + 5 N right = 15 N right
  • Opposite directions → subtract, toward the bigger side: 30 N right, 25 N left → 5 N right
  • Net force 0 N = balanced → motion does NOT change (stays at rest or keeps the same speed and direction)
  • Net force not 0 = unbalanced → motion changes (starts, stops, speeds up, slows down, turns)

Newton's 1st Law (Law of Inertia)

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at the same speed and direction, unless an unbalanced force acts on it.

  • Inertia = resisting changes in motion. More mass = more inertia.
  • Seatbelt: the car stops, but your body keeps moving forward
  • Objects slow down because of friction, not because they "run out of force"

Thermal Energy Transfer 7.8A

TypeHow it movesExamples
Conductiondirect contact, particle to particlepan handle gets hot; hand on ice
Convectioncurrents in liquids/gases: warm rises, cool sinksboiling water; oven air; sea breeze
Radiationwaves, no contact or matter neededsunlight; campfire warmth; heat lamp
  • Conductors (metals) transfer thermal energy quickly. Insulators (wood, plastic, air, foam) slow it down.
  • Convection: warm fluid is less dense, so it rises; cooler fluid sinks, making a loop called a current.
  • Radiation can travel through empty space (that's how the Sun warms Earth).

Temperature & Equilibrium 7.8B, 7.8C

  • Thermal energy always moves from warmer → cooler until both reach the same temperature (thermal equilibrium)
  • "Cold" does not move. Ice feels cold because energy leaves your hand.
  • 70 °C spoon in 20 °C water → final temperature is between 20 and 70 °C
  • Temperature = average kinetic energy (motion) of particles. Hotter = particles move faster.

Solutions 7.6D

  • Solute = what dissolves (salt, sugar). Solvent = what it dissolves in (water). Aqueous = in water.
  • Concentration = how much solute in an amount of solvent. More solute OR less water → more concentrated
  • Dilution = adding more solvent (water). The solute amount stays the same → less concentrated
  • Compare: 20 g in 100 mL is more concentrated than 20 g in 200 mL

Rate of Dissolution 7.6E

Speeds it upWhy (particles)
Higher temperaturewater particles move faster and hit the solute harder and more often
More surface area (crushed)more solute is exposed, so more water particles touch it at once
Agitation (stirring)brings fresh water to the solute and carries dissolved particles away

Fair test: change ONE variable (independent) and keep the others the same (controlled). Measure the time to dissolve (dependent).

Shorter time = faster dissolving. Fastest: hot water + crushed + stirred. Slowest: cold water + whole cube + no stirring.

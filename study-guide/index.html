Speed & Velocity 7.7A, 7.7B

speed = distance ÷ time   distance = speed × time   time = distance ÷ speed

  • 50 m in 10 s → 50 ÷ 10 = 5 m/s  ·  3 m/s for 8 s → 24 m  ·  20 m at 2 m/s → 10 s
  • Average speed for a whole trip = total distance ÷ total time (don't average the speeds). Always include units!
  • Velocity = speed + direction: "270 mph west" is a velocity. "180 miles in 3 hours" is only a speed.
  • Turning at the same speed → the velocity changes (direction changed).
  • Distance = total path. Displacement = start to finish, with direction. 30 m east, then 10 m west → distance 40 m, displacement 20 m east. One full lap → displacement 0 m.

Distance-Time Graphs 7.7C

  • Straight sloped line = constant speed. Steeper slope = greater speed.
  • Speed in a segment = change in distance ÷ change in time (rose 20 m in 4 s → 5 m/s).
  • Graph from a table: time on the x-axis, distance on the y-axis, plot each pair, connect the points, and add a title, labels and units.

Forces & Net Force 6.7A, 6.7B

Types: applied (push/pull), friction (opposes motion), normal (surface pushes up), gravity (pulls down), magnetism. Force is measured in newtons (N).

  • Same direction → add: 10 N right + 5 N right = 15 N right
  • Opposite directions → subtract, toward the bigger side. Tug-of-war: 105 N left vs. 150 N right → 45 N right, unbalanced
  • Net force 0 N = balanced. Net force not 0 = unbalanced.
  • To balance 30 N right vs. 45 N left, add 15 N to the right.

Newton's 1st Law & Inertia 7.7D

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at the same speed and direction, unless an unbalanced force acts on it.

  • Balanced forces → motion does NOT change: the object stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant speed in a straight line.
  • Unbalanced forces → motion changes: starts, stops, speeds up, slows down or turns.
  • Inertia = resisting changes in motion. More mass = more inertia.
  • Demos: coin drops into the cup, tablecloth trick, seatbelt (your body keeps moving forward). Things slow down because of friction, not because they "run out of force."

1st Six Weeks Review 7.6A–E

  • Element = one kind of atom (Fe, O₂, He). Compound = two or more different elements chemically combined (H₂O, CO₂, NaCl).
  • Counting atoms: the subscript counts the atom before it (no subscript = 1). H₂SO₄ → H 2 + S 1 + O 4 = 7 atoms.
  • Physical change = same substance (melting, cutting, dissolving, boiling). Chemical change = new substance. Signs: new gas/bubbles, color change, temperature change, light, new solid, new odor (burning, rusting, baking).
  • Solute dissolves (salt, sugar). Solvent does the dissolving (water). More solute or less water = more concentrated. Adding water = dilution (the solute amount stays the same).
  • Dissolves faster with higher temperature, more surface area (crushed) and agitation (stirring) because more water particles hit the solute.
  • Fair test: change ONE variable and keep the others the same. Shorter time = faster.

Key Vocabulary from our unit word walls

Motion: change in position compared to a reference point · Linear: in a straight line · Cardinal directions: north, east, south, west · Slope: steepness of a graph's line · Constant speed: the same speed the whole time · Contact force: happens when objects touch (applied, friction, normal) · Resting position: staying in the same spot relative to another object · Net force: total of all forces after adding/subtracting · Inertia: resistance to a change in motion
